\item[\textbf{Problem 7}]
Prove that $a = n - 1$ is always a solution to $a^{2} \equiv 1 \bmod n$.

\Solution

We want to show that if \(a = n-1\), then
\[
    a^2 \equiv 1 \bmod n.
\]
Substituting \(a = n-1\), it suffices to prove that
\[
    (n-1)^2 \equiv 1 \bmod n.
\]

Expanding the square gives
\[
    (n-1)^2 = n^2 - 2n + 1.
\]
Now reduce modulo \(n\):
\[
    n^2 - 2n + 1 \equiv 0 - 0 + 1 \equiv 1 \bmod n,
\]
since both \(n^2\) and \(2n\) are multiples of \(n\).

Therefore,
\[
    (n-1)^2 \equiv 1 \bmod n,
\]
so \(a = n-1\) is always a solution to \(a^2 \equiv 1 \bmod n\).
\(\blacksquare\)
