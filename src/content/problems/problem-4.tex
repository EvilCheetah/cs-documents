\item[\textbf{Problem 4}]
A company is selling an artifact worth millions of dollars. Many interested
buyers are in the bidding process, which works as follows. Each buyer sends the
information of his/her final number (e.g., \FormatCurrency{1,000,000}) to the
company to show his/her commitment. And the number must be kept confidential
from other buyers and even the company before the deadline. After the deadline,
all numbers are released to all buyers and the company, then the buyer with the
highest number wins. Design a HASH FUNCTION based scheme to secure the bidding
process. Describe steps a buyer and/or the company should do.


\Solution

To ensure the bidding process is secure, confidential, and verifiable, the
system should implement a Cryptographic Commitment Scheme consisting of a
``Commit'' phase and a ``Reveal'' phase.

\subsection{Phase 1: Commit}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item An interested buyer decides on their bid amount ($B$). They then
    generate a large, cryptographically secure random number, salt or nonce
    ($K$).
    
    \item The buyer hashes their bid amount combined with the nonce: $C = H(B,
    K)$. This resulting hash ($C$) is their "commitment".

    \item The buyer sends only their commitment ($C$) alongside their unique
    identification (such as, system ID, full name, driver's license) to the
    company.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Phase 2: Reveal}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Once the deadline passes, all buyers publicly release their original
    plaintext bid amount ($B$) and their secret nonce ($K$) to the company and
    all other buyers.

    \item The company (and any participating buyer) verifies the bids by
    recomputing the hash using the revealed data: $H(B, K)$
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[ colback=black!5!white, colframe=black!75!white, title=Note ]
    To ensure no bias during the selection process based on buyer
    identification, the company could implement a blinded approach where the
    buyer's ID is also hashed or anonymized during the Commit phase.
    Furthermore, to protect network-level privacy, buyers could submit their
    commitments via a reverse proxy or an anonymity network (like Tor/onion
    routing) to hide their IP addresses, ensuring that the company cannot
    identify or profile bidders prior to the Reveal phase.
\end{tcolorbox}
