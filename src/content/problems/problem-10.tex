\item[\textbf{Problem 10}]
An even trickier question: In a three-message authentication protocol, Alice
initiates contact with Bob. Bob is a stateless server, and thus it is
inconvenient for him to remember the challenges sent to Alice. Assume that Alice
and Bob share a key $K_{Alice-Bob}$ and Bob also has a key $K_{Bob}$ only known
to himself. The protocol works as follows, where $R_1$ and $R_2$ are random
numbers generated by Alice and Bob, respectively.

\Solution

\subsubsection*{%
    Does this protocol provide mutual authentication? If yes, why? If no, who
    cannot authenticate whom and why%
}
\textbf{Yes, under normal, honest execution}. If we assume no active attackers
are interfering, the protocol is theoretically designed to achieve mutual
authentication.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Alice authenticates Bob: In the first message, Alice sends a fresh
    random challenge, $R_1$. In the second message, Bob replies with
    $K_{Alice-Bob}(R_1+R_2)$. Because this ciphertext contains Alice's fresh
    $R_1$ (modified by adding $R_2$) and is encrypted with their shared secret
    key, Alice has proof that the sender possesses the key and is actively
    responding to her specific session.
    
    \item Bob authenticates Alice: Bob sends his own challenge, $R_2$, hidden
    inside the encrypted state $K_{Bob}(R_2)$ and the shared encryption
    $K_{Alice-Bob}(R_1+R_2)$. To successfully generate the final message,
    $K_{Alice-Bob}(R_2)$, Alice must possess the shared key to decrypt the
    payload, extract $R_2$, and re-encrypt it. When Bob receives this, he
    verifies it using his stateless ticket, proving Alice holds the key
\end{enumerate}


\subsubsection*{%
    Could a third person, Trudy, impersonate Alice? Describe a possible
    attack scenario. If it is not possible, clearly state so and give reasons.%
}
\textbf{Yes}. Trudy can easily impersonate Alice by exploiting how Bob generates
his response, effectively turning Bob into an encryption oracle. This is a
classic chosen-plaintext attack.

Attack scenario:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The Exploit: The protocol dictates that Bob takes Alice's $R_1$, adds
    his $R_2$, and encrypts the result.
    
    \item The Attack: Trudy initiates contact with Bob, claiming to be Alice.
    However, instead of sending a random number for $R_1$, she deliberately
    sends $R_1 = 0$.

    \item Bob's Oracle Response: Bob receives $0$, generates his random $R_2$,
    and computes $0 + R_2$, which is simply $R_2$. He then replies to Trudy with
    $K_{Bob}(R_2)$ and $K_{Alice-Bob}(R_2)$.

    \item The Impersonation: Trudy now holds exactly what she needs for the
    third message. She simply echoes Bob's message back to him: $K_{Bob}(R_2)$
    and $K_{Alice-Bob}(R_2)$.

    \item Result: Bob decrypts his state ticket, checks the payload, and
    successfully authenticates Trudy as Alice, even though Trudy has no
    knowledge of the shared key $K_{Alice-Bob}$.
\end{enumerate}


\subsubsection*{%
    Could Trudy impersonate Bob? Describe a possible attack scenario. If
    it is not possible, clearly state so and give reasons.%
}
\textbf{Yes}. Trudy can impersonate Bob to Alice by using an interleaving/relay
attack (a variation of the reflection attacks discussed in your lecture notes).
Because the encrypted payloads do not explicitly bind the messages to a specific
sender or direction, Trudy can use the real Bob to solve Alice's challenges for
her.

Attack scenario:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Interception: Alice initiates a legitimate session, sending
    \texttt{I'm Alice}, $R_1$ to Trudy (believing Trudy is Bob).
    
    \item Relay (Session 2): Trudy pauses Alice, turns around, and initiates a
    new parallel session with the real Bob, sending him Alice's exact message:
    \texttt{I'm Alice}, $R_1$.

    \item Bob's Honest Response: The real Bob processes this normally,
    generating his own $R_2$, and replies to Trudy with $K_{Bob}(R_2)$ and
    $K_{Alice-Bob}(R_1 + R_2)$.

    \item The Impersonation: Trudy forwards this exact message back to Alice in
    their original session.

    \item Result: Alice decrypts the payload, verifies her $R_1$ is present, and
    falsely concludes she is communicating securely with Bob. Trudy has
    successfully impersonated Bob without ever knowing the keys.
\end{enumerate}
