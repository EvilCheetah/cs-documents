\item[\textbf{Problem 1}]
Suppose that for inputs of size $n$ on a particular computer, insertion sort
runs in $8n^2$ steps and merge sort runs in $64n \lg n$ steps. For which
values of $n$ does insertion sort beat merge sort?

\Solution

To compare the efficiency of these two algorithms, their performance functions
are plotted in Figure \ref{figure:insertion-vs-merge}. Merge sort is represented
by the blue line and insertion sort by the red line.

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{exercise-1.2-2-graph.pdf}
    \captionof{figure}{Comparison of Insertion Sort vs. Merge Sort}
    \label{figure:insertion-vs-merge}
\end{minipage}

The graph indicates that for small values of $n$, insertion sort performs fewer
steps than merge sort. To find the exact crossing point, we solve for $n$ where
the two functions intersect:

\begin{alignat*}{2}
                & \quad & 8n^2                &= 64n \cdot \log_{2}(n)                 \\
    \Rightarrow & \quad & \cancel{8n} \cdot n &= \cancel{8n} \cdot 8 \cdot \log_{2}(n) \\
    \Rightarrow & \quad & n                   &= 8 \cdot \log_{2}(n)                   \\
    \Rightarrow & \quad & \frac{n}{8}         &= \log_{2}(n)                           \\
    \Rightarrow & \quad & 2^{\frac{n}{8}}     &= n                                     \\
\end{alignat*}

Solving this equation for $n$ (either via numerical methods or a
calculator) results in an intersection at approximately $n \approx 43.55926$.
Therefore, for $2 \leq n \leq 43$, insertion sort is more efficient. For $n \geq
44$, merge sort becomes a better choice.
